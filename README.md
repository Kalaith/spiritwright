# Spiritwright

A 2D, browser-playable PvE auto-battler built in Godot 4.2.2. Collect spirits, build golem vessels for them from frames, arms, legs and sigils, arrange a team of up to six on a 3x2 board and watch a deterministic battle decide whether your theory works. Design: `Spiritwright_Game_Design.md`.

## What is in this build (GDD section 10 vertical slice)

- **28 collectable spirits**: Pyra (Fire, Brash), Neris (Water, Calm) and Odo (Earth, Stubborn) to start, Briar, Volta and Sefa rescued in the campaign, Vesper from the Crucible, and every one of the 21 wild spirits can be recruited too. Each has its own base stats, growth rates, a trait, a temperament preference and an awakening. A recruitable wild spirit is a `wild` entry with a `title`, `temperament`, `description` and `awakening`; as an enemy it still fights in its wild form (no awakening, no temperament: `Builds.resolve(..., wild = true)`), so recruiting changes no stage's balance. The collection splits roughly 60/30/10 by how hard a spirit is to bring in:
  - **16 found by playing** (59%): the three starters and 13 first-clear stage rewards, about two per region (Rubble Imp, Moss Wisp, Briar in the Ruins; Bog Lurker, Reed Sprite, Volta in the Fen; Ash Hound, Dust Devil, Sefa on the Steppe; Gale Kite, Frost Wisp in the Frostspine; Storm Hawk, Thunder Drake on the Storm Crown).
  - **8 from challenges** (30%): Cinder Mote, Brine Sprite, Root Warden, Spark Newt, Fen Mother, Slag Brute, Rime Ogre and Volt Shade, each from one stage challenge where it is fought.
  - **4 from extreme feats** (14%): the Kiln Tyrant (Trial by Fire: beat the Great Kiln with a single golem), the Crag Sovereign (Skeleton Crew: a whole playthrough never fielding more than three golems), the Storm Sovereign (Unbroken: a whole playthrough, every stage, boss and the Storm Crown, without a golem of yours ever falling) and Vesper (Boss Royale: complete the Crucible's Classic Royale).
  - **Vesper, the Crucible Spirit** (Echo, Restless) has no home material: the Echo element reacts a little with every material (Echoing Stone, Grove, Frost, Bell, Prism, Mire, Shard and Spark). Its trait, Adaptation, gives a modest stat bonus that depends on its frame (a trait's `by_frame`), and Restless spirits prefer a golem built differently from their last battle (`prefers_new_build`, judged on each spirit's remembered `last_build`).
- **Codex challenges**: two on every stage (60), in each region file (`data/regions/<id>.json`) as a stage's `challenges` list (`id`, `name`, `text`, `rules`, `rewards`, optional `"extreme": true`). Any win whose golems keep every rule meets one, first clear or replay, and it stays met across playthroughs. Rules are a fixed vocabulary checked against the finished battle's golems (`challenges.gd`): `no_falls`, `front_stands` (no front-row golem falls), `max_golems`, `forbid_elements`, `only_elements`, `only_materials`, `forbid_arms`, `attack_kind`, `empty_row`, `no_sigils`, `require` (some golem matches every given field: spirit, element, frame, arms, legs, material, sigil, row), `max_level` and `min_cycle`. The scouting report lists a stage's challenges, the result screen names the ones met and the ones still open.
- **Region mastery**: meeting every challenge in a region (Trial by Fire included on the Steppe) masters it and unlocks that region's sigil, found nowhere else: Warden's Seal (Ruins: team Ward at the start, team Regen when hurt), Mire Lantern (Fen: Soak and arc), Kiln Heart (Steppe: deep Burn, kills hurry the team), Rime Crown (Frostspine: slows every enemy at the start, attacks chill) and Tempest Eye (Storm Crown: lightning on every enemy each turn, attacks Expose). A region's optional `mastery` in its region file holds `name`, `text` and part `rewards`; a save that already met them all gets the reward on load.
- **Feats**: rules on a whole playthrough (`feats` in `campaign.json`), judged the moment its last stage is cleared, against the playthrough record: `no_falls`, `no_losses`, `max_golems` (most fielded in one battle) and `min_cycle`. The result screen says when a battle puts a feat out of reach for the rest of the playthrough.
- **The Codex** (from the map): every spirit with how to find it, every challenge by region (pick a stage to go there), and the feats with this playthrough's record.
- **Resting spirits learn by watching**: spirits not in a golem earn `xp.resting` (half) of what a battle would give them, so the bench keeps pace and any recruit can be fielded without grinding.
- **Awakenings**: at Lv 5 every spirit grows a second trait (Pyra's Wildfire, Neris's Floodwall, Odo's Rumble, Sefa's Tailwind, Volta's Overcharge, Briar's Bloom). The roster shows what each will become; the result screen announces it.
- **4 frames** (Bulwark, Strider, Conduit, Colossus), **11 arms** (Hammer, Shield, Spirit Cannon, Bow, Aegis, Pike, Lantern, Chain Flail, Twin Blades, Storm Staff, Reaper Scythe), **6 legs** (Pillar, Spider, Tower, Stilts, Crawler Treads, Ice Skates) and **16 sigils** (Ember Sigil, Guard Bell, Surge Lens, Moss Charm, Stoneskin Rune, Mending Rune, Leech Fang, Thorn Crest, War Drum, Hunter's Brand, Frost Sigil, Vengeance Idol, Cleansing Charm, Hex Eye, Storm Coil, Phoenix Ember). Every part changes behaviour, not just stats. The later parts bring their own mechanics: armour pierce (Pike), splash onto row neighbours (Flail), two hits per turn (Twin Blades), execute below half HP (Scythe), heal-on-hit (Lantern), a back-row damage bonus (Stilts), status immunity (Treads, Colossus), life drain, thorns, slowing, Weaken and Expose, cleansing, a team surge when an ally falls and a once-per-battle revive. The defensive options: Shield Arms intercept, Aegis Arms ward their lane every turn, Tower Legs brace the front row (-25% attack damage), the Stoneskin Rune hardens when struck and the Mending Rune heals the most wounded ally each turn.
- **8 body materials**: Stone and Wood from the start, Ice (Sunken Court), Crystal (Glimmer Shards), Bogwood (Fen Mother), Bronze (Cinder Kiln), Obsidian (Kiln Tyrant) and Fulgurite (Charged Vault). Each shifts stats and changes the golem's look. Some react with the spirit's element: Kindling (Fire in Wood: high STR/MAG, almost no DEF/RES, singes attackers), Steam (Fire in Ice), Glacier (Water in Ice), Heartwood (Nature in Wood), Living Rock (Earth in Stone), Live Wire (Lightning in Bronze), Wind Chime (Wind in Crystal), Mangrove and Bog Spring (Nature / Water in Bogwood), Magma Heart and Basalt (Fire / Earth in Obsidian), Thunderglass and Singing Glass (Lightning / Wind in Fulgurite). Wild spirits use them too: the lead cinder motes are kindling, the brine sprite is ice and the Root Warden is heartwood.
- **Up to 6v6 automated battles** on a 3x2 board per side (three lanes, front and back rows) with Burn, Soaked, Ward, Stun, Taunt, Regen, Weaken and Expose, interceptions, chained damage and a readable combat log.
- **One golem to start**: the campaign opens with a single golem (Pyra in a kindling body) while Neris and Odo rest. The Mossy Gate and Sunken Court each yield a **golem core**, and the first stage of the Fen, Steppe and Frostspine one more, so the team is three golems through the Ruins, four in the Fen, five on the Steppe and six from the Frostspine on; spend each core in the **Golem Laboratory** to build another body for a resting spirit. Enemy teams match yours: one at the Gate, then three, four, five and six per region (the first stage of a region is fought before its core arrives).
- **Five regions, 30 stages**, each drawn as an illustrated map with a landmark for every stage, fog over places not yet reached, and your golems walking to whichever stage is selected; `<` and `>` step between regions you have reached. The **Ancient Ruins** (Lv 1-5, the Root Warden), **Glimmer Fen** (Lv 5-9, the Fen Mother), **Ember Steppe** (Lv 7-13, the Kiln Tyrant), **Frostspine Pass** (Lv 13-17, the Crag Sovereign) and **Storm Crown** (Lv 16-20, the Storm Sovereign) bring 21 wild spirits. Part and spirit rewards are spread through every region: an ordinary stage gives at most one or two, a boss two to four. Later wild spirits field the new parts too. Each region has one optional branch stage.
- **XP from the fight, not a flat amount**: the enemies' average level times `xp.per_stage_level`, scaled by first clear / replay / loss, and by how far the spirit's level sits below or above that average (`xp.level_gap_step`, clamped to `xp.min_factor`-`xp.max_factor`). Each level costs `xp_per_level + xp_per_level_growth x (level - 1)` up to `level_cap` 50. Costs and XP both grow linearly with level, and the strong level-gap factor pulls spirits toward the stage levels, so playing each stage once keeps pace without grinding. The campaign ends around Lv 20; the rest is for New Game+.
- **New Game+**: once the Storm Crown (the stage marked `"finale": true`) falls, the map offers New Game+. Every stage is fought again, and its first-clear rewards are given again (parts and spirits you own are skipped, and cores only fill empty cradles). Wild spirits are `new_game_plus.level_step` (15) levels stronger per cycle, up to `level_cap`. Spirits, levels, parts and golems carry over. Each playthrough keeps a record of golems fallen, battles not won and the most golems fielded in one battle.
- **The Shifting Crucible** (from the map once the Storm Crown has fallen): a boss gauntlet for one locked team. Beginning a run locks the laboratory team as it stands (up to six golems, spirits, parts, materials, sigils and cells) for six encounters and a final; health and statuses reset between encounters, one defeat or timeout ends the run, and the next run starts again from the first encounter. Every golem on both sides fights at the tier's level (Adept: yours Lv 20, enemies 2 above; Master, opened by Boss Royale: Lv 30 and 8 above), each enemy also carrying its own `offset`, so the test is the team's composition, not its levels. Crucible battles give no XP and take nothing. Each encounter tests something different (Sustain, Defence, Retaliation, Reach, Status, Protection, Speed, Control) with existing bosses and wild spirits; the whole sequence can be scouted, enemy by enemy, before a run begins. The **Classic Royale** is a fixed sequence; a **Shifting Royale** draws six of the eight encounters, their order, a variant of each court and one of two finals from its seed (shown, and kept for the next attempt). Records keep the best step reached, runs, completions and the last five winning teams per Royale and tier. **Milestones** (`milestones` in `crucible.json`, rules `reach`, `complete`, `mode`, `tier`, `max_golems`) bring Vesper, titles shown on the map and cosmetic **vessel finishes** any golem can wear (a recoloured trim and a halo; they never change a fight). Encounters beaten are Codex entries in the Codex's Crucible tab.
- **Persistent saves** (IndexedDB in the browser, `user://` on desktop) through the toolkit's JsonSave: staged writes, a backup, recovery from it, a damaged or newer save kept rather than overwritten, and the old save kept as `.previous` when a new game starts.

## Prototype answers to the GDD's open questions

| Question | Decision for this slice |
| --- | --- |
| Combat representation | Side view, two teams of up to six, each golem in one of six cells: three lanes (top to bottom) by two rows. |
| Battle cadence | Timed initiative: gauges fill by SPD each tick; arms set the action cost (Hammer slow, Shield fast). |
| Spirit acquisition | Three tiers: first-clear stage rewards, stage challenges and extreme feats (about 60/30/10). New recruits join at the team's average level. |
| Growth variance | Smoothed, not rolled: each level adds the growth % to a running total and every 100 is +1. No unlucky spirits. |
| Arms vs weapon | Arms define the attack; there is no separate weapon slot. One sigil slot. |
| Rebuild economy | Free. Unlocked parts are blueprints usable by any number of golems. |
| Team size | Up to 6 (`team_size`, at most the six cells). The campaign starts with one golem; `"cores"` stage rewards let the laboratory build more. Save schema 4 adds unspent `cores`; schema 3 saves keep their three golems. Schema 5 adds each golem's `lane`; older saves fill each row's centre lane first. Schema 6 adds the New Game+ `cycle` and the playthrough `run` record; a schema 5 save already under way starts with a spoiled record. Schema 7 adds the met `challenges` and `feats` (empty for older saves). Schema 8 adds the `crucible` block (run in progress, records, milestones, encounters beaten, title, last run), each spirit's `last_build` and an optional cosmetic `finish` per golem. Migrations and validation live in `save_schema.gd`. |
| Affinity | Both: universal status rules (Soaked puts out Burn, blocks new Burn, slows and takes +30% magic damage) plus per-spirit traits. |
| Compatibility | Simple temperament bonuses only (no Resonance yet). Tags (Brash, Calm, Stubborn), a new frame (Curious), the back row (Patient), an ally from the last battle (Loyal) or a changed build (Restless). |
| Materials | One per golem (`material` in a loadout), from `parts.json` `materials`. Reactions are data: `reactions.<element>` holds stats and effects in the same vocabulary as traits. Save schema 3 adds materials; schema 2 saves rebuild every golem in stone and unlock what the start and cleared stages grant. |
| Unlocked traits | One Awakening per spirit at `balance.awaken_level` (5): a second data-driven trait, never a stat penalty. |
| Visuals | Procedural 2D golems assembled from part silhouettes at runtime (`golem_frames.gd`, `golem_legs.gd`, `golem_parts.gd`), each frame with its own head, shaded and textured by material (cracks, grain, sheen, rivets, facets); the element shows as core and eye colour plus a letter; reactions add an animated aura (`golem_fx.gd`). Battles play in front of a drawn ruins backdrop; bosses (`"boss": true` on a wild spirit) stand larger. |

Targeting rules: each arms' attack has an `aim` row. Hammer, Shield, Aegis and the Spirit Cannon aim at the front row, Bow Arms at the back row; the enemy in that row whose lane is nearest the attacker's is struck, and the other row is only reached once the aimed row is empty. So a hammer cannot touch the back row while anyone stands in front. Spider Legs ignore this and hunt the lowest-HP enemy anywhere. A Taunting enemy overrides everything (nearest Taunter first). Melee from the back row strikes at `battle.back_row_melee_factor` (half) power, except on Spider Legs. Front-row Shield Arms take one hit aimed at a back-row ally in their own or a neighbouring lane between their turns.

Effect vocabulary (traits, awakenings, sigils, reactions and parts, validated by `catalog.gd`): triggers `battle_start`, `turn_start`, `hit`, `hurt`, `low_hp`, `kill`, `ally_death` (a teammate falls) and `fatal` (a blow would destroy the owner; fires once); actions `status` (burn, soaked, taunt, regen, stun, weaken, expose), `ward`, `heal`, `damage`, `haste` (action gauge; a negative amount slows), `chain` and `drain` (on hit, a `fraction` of the damage), `cleanse` (clears Burn, Soaked, Weaken, Expose and Stun) and `revive` (on `fatal`, to `self`: HP set to a `fraction` of max); receivers `self`, `target` (on hit), `attacker` (on hurt), `team`, `allies`, `enemies`, `lane` (the owner and whoever shares its lane) and `wounded` (the ally or owner with the lowest share of HP, if anyone is hurt). Frames, arms and legs may all carry `effects`. Arms attacks take optional `pierce` (armour ignored), `splash` (share dealt to the target's row neighbours), `execute` (extra power against targets below half HP) and `strikes` (blows per action). Legs may carry a `brace` fraction (front-row damage taken), a `perch` fraction (back-row damage dealt) and, like frames, an `immune` list of statuses. Weaken and Expose strengths are `status.weaken_fraction` and `status.expose_fraction`. Amounts are `flat` plus `stat` x `fraction` of the owner's stat.

## Adding regions and stages

The campaign is all data: `data/campaign.json` holds the start, the feats and the regions in order, and each region (its terrain, stages, challenges and mastery) lives in `data/regions/<id>.json`; `catalog.gd` validates it at startup and refuses to run with a readable error if anything is wrong.

- **A region** is an entry in `regions` (shown in list order): `id`, `name`, `text`, a `laboratory` (`name`, `map_pos`: the start of its trails, and a button into the laboratory) and a `terrain` block: `seed`, hex colours `ground`, `dirt`, `rock`, `wall`, `water`, colour lists `patches` and `canopy`, and layout lists `stream` (points; `[]` for none), `walls` (`[start, end, height]`) and `woods` (`[centre, radius]`).
- **A stage** goes in its region's `stages`: unique `id`, `name`, `text`, `map_pos`, `landmark`, `requires` (stage ids, from any region), `seed`, 1-6 `enemies` (loadouts with a `level` and a `row` + `lane` 0-2, each in its own cell; any spirit from `spirits.json`), `rewards` (`{}` for XP only), optional `"boss": true` and optional `clear_text` shown on the first win.
- Map positions are in an 860x560 space; landmarks rise about 110 px above the marker and the name sits below it. Landmarks: `gate`, `court`, `stair`, `cloister`, `vault`, `heart`, `bridge`, `hut`, `tower`, `kiln`, `spire`, `camp`, `monolith`, `peak`.
- A region opens once any of its stages is reachable, so its first stage should require the previous region's boss. Trails from stages in other regions start at the laboratory.
- A stage may list `challenges` (see Codex challenges above); `"finale": true` marks the one stage whose fall completes a playthrough. `sim_checks.gd` checks that every playable spirit joins exactly one way and that the collection stays near 60/30/10.
- New wild spirits go in `spirits.json` `wild` using the effect vocabulary below; give one a `title`, `temperament`, `description` and `awakening` to make it recruitable, and a source (a stage, challenge or feat reward). Run the checks and `-- --report` after adding stages to see the level curve and win rates; the report also lists how often random builds that keep each challenge's composition rules meet it, now and ten levels on (25 for an extreme one).
- **XP rates** are whole-number percentages: `balance.json` `xp.gain_percent` scales every spirit's XP (80 = 20% less), and an optional `xp_percent` on a spirit in `spirits.json` scales that spirit on top of it (100 when absent).

## Adding Crucible encounters

The Crucible is all data in `data/crucible.json`, validated by `crucible_rules.gd` at startup.

- An **encounter** in `encounters` (or a **final** in `finals`) has `id`, `name`, `test` (the one word the scouting shows), `seed`, `text` and `variants`: one or more enemy teams of 1-6 loadouts, each with an `offset` added to the tier's level plus its `enemy_bonus`. A Classic Royale always fights variant 0; a Shifting Royale picks one by seed.
- `classic` lists `encounters_per_run` encounter ids and a `final`. A Shifting Royale draws from every encounter and final, so adding one widens it without touching the Classic.
- A **tier** has `id`, `name`, `level`, `enemy_bonus`, `text` and an optional `requires` (a milestone id). Milestones reward `spirits`, `titles` and `finishes` (each `finishes` entry has a `color`).
- Run `-- --crucible` after any change: for every encounter and variant it prints the win rate of 150 random six-golem teams at Adept, the parts most over- and under-represented among the wins (what the encounter asks for), how many of the seven each team would win one by one (a flat spread means the encounters test different things), how far they get in a real run, and a few teams that finished. The checks fight hand-built teams of three different shapes through the Classic Royale and expect each to finish, and a team of many small burning blows to fall at the Bellows.

## Layout

```
data/                 catalogs: spirits, parts, campaign (+ regions/, one file per region), balance, crucible
scenes/main.tscn      single scene; scripts/main.gd swaps screens
scripts/core/         catalog (load + validate), campaign_rules (campaign.json validation), builds (stat resolution, growth),
                      experience (XP and levels), challenges (challenge and feat rules), progression (save state),
                      save_schema (save validation and migrations), crucible (gauntlet rules), crucible_rules
                      (crucible.json validation), crucible_record (the Crucible's saved state)
scripts/sim/          battle_sim.gd: deterministic simulation and event log
scripts/ui/           title, campaign map (terrain, landmarks, wild landmarks), golem laboratory, battle playback and backdrop, results, the Codex, the Crucible (screen, records, results), enemy cards, golem art and reaction fx
addons/hatchery/      the shared WebHatchery Godot toolkit, linked from ../godot_toolkit (not committed): saves,
                      JSON and data rules, balance lookups, seeded picks, screen switching, check and capture runners
tools/                sim_checks.gd (headless checks on the toolkit's check runner, + balance report), capture.gd
                      (screenshots, on the toolkit's capture runner), checks/ (check_module: the shared catalog and
                      battle helpers; checks_progression, checks_battle, checks_collection, checks_crucible: the
                      scenarios; balance_report: the campaign report)
```

## Running and checking

The game loads the shared toolkit (`../godot_toolkit`) as `res://addons/hatchery/`. After cloning, link it once (a directory junction, not committed here):

```powershell
..\godot_toolkit\tools\Link-Toolkit.ps1 -ProjectDir .
```

```powershell
.\Run.ps1                                     # run in the editor's game window
$godot = '..\small_following\exports\tooling\Godot_v4.2.2-stable_win64_console.exe'
& $godot --headless --path . --script res://tools/sim_checks.gd               # checks
& $godot --headless --path . --script res://tools/sim_checks.gd -- --only=save # scenarios whose name contains "save"
& $godot --headless --path . --script res://tools/sim_checks.gd -- --report   # + balance report (campaign and Crucible, ~1.5 min)
& $godot --headless --path . --script res://tools/sim_checks.gd -- --crucible # + the Crucible's balance report alone
& $godot --path . --script res://tools/capture.gd -- C:\temp\captures        # screenshots of every screen
```

## Publishing

`publish.ps1` and `publish-itch.ps1` are thin wrappers around the shared publisher in `../godot_toolkit/publishing/`. `.\publish.ps1 -BuildOnly` exports Web and Windows builds into `builds/publish/`; `.\publish.ps1` also copies the web build to `PREVIEW_ROOT/games/spiritwright` (served at `http://127.0.0.1/games/spiritwright/`) and records the deployment in Project Roost; `-Production` / `-FTP` target production; `publish-itch.ps1` pushes to itch.io. The web build needs the COOP/COEP headers in the toolkit's `web.htaccess`, deployed beside it; a game's own `tools/publishing/web.htaccess` would override it. `-DryRun` changes nothing.
